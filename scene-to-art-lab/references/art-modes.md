## Transparent watercolor

**Best for:** travel, architecture, plants, quiet portraits, changing weather, reflective scenes, and luminous products.

**Composition moves:** reserve untouched paper for light; concentrate pigment around the focal anchor; let selected contours disappear into atmosphere; use one or two directional washes to connect masses. End peripheral forms in clear-water blooms, lifted pigment, dry-brush gaps, or untouched paper rather than a soft digital fade.

**Material grammar:** cold-press cotton paper, transparent glazing, wet-on-wet diffusion, dry-brush accents, granulation, pigment pooling, cauliflower blooms used sparingly, lifted highlights, visible pencil only when structurally useful.

**Color logic:** use 3–5 transparent pigments; let mixtures occur optically; protect luminous neutrals; use one concentrated chromatic note near the focus.

**Avoid:** uniform blur, pastel digital haze, opaque airbrush, equal detail everywhere, random rainbow splashes, heavy black outlines, paper texture pasted over the whole image.

## Pop screenprint

**Best for:** portraits, cars, products, street scenes, fashion, bold architecture, energetic social imagery.

**Composition moves:** separate the source into 3–6 value/color shapes; enlarge or repeat one identity anchor; use hard crops and graphic diagonals; let one offset layer create motion; keep a decisive focal silhouette. Make secondary edges vanish through decreasing halftone density, broken stencil shapes, ink starvation, or a color plate merging into the paper—not blur.

**Material grammar:** flat ink layers, coarse halftone, stencil edges, selective overprint, slight registration offset, uneven ink coverage, paper tooth, limited repetition.

**Color logic:** use 3–5 high-contrast inks plus paper; assign each color a role; allow one complementary clash; preserve skin/product readability through value separation.

**Avoid:** generic comic speech bubbles, fake brand logos, uncontrolled neon, full-image Ben-Day dots, too many repeated panels, illegible facial or product geometry, decorative rectangles unrelated to the source.

## Expressive painting

**Best for:** motion, performance, landscape, night scenes, emotional portraits, dramatic vehicles, and light-driven architecture.

**Composition moves:** build large value masses first; direct brush flow along pose, road, wind, or light; sharpen one focal passage; allow peripheral forms to merge; exaggerate color temperature rather than anatomy. Lose secondary contours through scumbled ground, wiped passages, thin underpainting, broken strokes, or adjacent paint of similar value.

**Material grammar:** visible bristle direction, loaded strokes, scraped passages, thin underpainting, selective impasto, broken color, wet-into-wet joins, exposed ground.

**Color logic:** choose a dominant temperature and a smaller counter-temperature; mix neutrals from palette colors; reserve the thickest and highest-chroma paint for the focal area.

**Avoid:** uniform impasto, melted anatomy, arbitrary swirls, muddy saturation, every edge outlined, photographic detail under a paint-texture overlay.

## Ink-wash

**Best for:** mist, mountains, trees, restrained portraits, architecture with strong rhythm, minimal objects, contemplative scenes.

**Composition moves:** organize the image through solid, dry, wet, and empty passages; let blank space carry air or distance; reduce the scene to a few tonal masses; use one calligraphic directional structure. Dissolve peripheral edges through fading ink load, feathered moisture, flying-white gaps, and complete return to untouched paper.

**Material grammar:** absorbent xuan-like paper, carbon-black tonal range, dry flying-white strokes, pooled wash, feathered diffusion, crisp bone lines used selectively, occasional mineral or seal-red accent only when requested.

**Color logic:** monochrome by default; permit one restrained accent drawn from the source; distinguish depth by ink density and edge moisture.

**Avoid:** gray fog over everything, decorative pseudo-calligraphy, automatic red seals, literal tracing, excessive bamboo/landscape clichés, equal mid-gray values.

## Relief print

**Best for:** strong silhouettes, industrial subjects, portraits, trees, animals, high-contrast urban scenes, dramatic storytelling.

**Composition moves:** reduce the source to interlocking black and paper shapes; preserve identity through contour not shading; use carved directional marks to describe volume; cluster texture around structure and clear it from quiet areas. Let secondary forms disappear by widening paper cuts, thinning gouge marks, broken ink transfer, or allowing paper white to interrupt the contour.

**Material grammar:** linocut or woodcut gouges, chipped edges, pressure variation, ink gaps, roller texture, occasional two-block color registration.

**Color logic:** black plus paper is the strongest default; add one block color only when it improves hierarchy or symbolism.

**Avoid:** fine photographic crosshatching everywhere, vector-clean edges, decorative distress, more than two colored blocks, loss of facial or vehicle silhouette.

## Editorial surrealism

**Best for:** conceptual portraits, memory, travel, technology, identity, environmental themes, product narratives, and poetic scenes.

**Composition moves:** preserve the factual source as a visual anchor; introduce one impossible but immediately readable transformation; align the transformation with an existing contour, reflection, shadow, opening, road, body gesture, or object function. Make disappearance obey the concept: convert an edge into light, shadow, atmosphere, another material, an opening, or negative space with believable junctions.

**Concept engine:** choose one relation—inside/outside, scale reversal, material substitution, temporal overlap, duplication, absence, portal, or shadow autonomy. Make that single relation carry the meaning.

**Material grammar:** visually believable lighting and perspective at the impossible junction; clean hierarchy; restrained collage seams or photographic continuity according to the concept.

**Color logic:** preserve source atmosphere or shift it once for narrative contrast; use color to distinguish reality from transformation without producing two unrelated images.

**Avoid:** symbol soup, floating objects without reason, generic clouds in heads, multiple unrelated metaphors, impossible lighting, gratuitous face distortion, decorative surrealism with no connection to the scene.
