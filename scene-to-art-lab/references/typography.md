# Default Poster Typography

Use this typography system for every generated poster unless the user explicitly requests another type direction or no text. Treat it as a consistent visual form, not as a claim that the image model can reproduce a licensed font file exactly.

## Locked visual form

- Use a modern condensed neo-grotesk sans serif with clean geometric construction.
- Set the primary title in bold or semibold ALL CAPS, tall and narrow, with tight but readable tracking.
- Keep terminals plain, strokes even, counters open, and the silhouette editorial rather than sporty, techno, ornamental, handwritten, or retro-futurist.
- Use strong scale contrast: one large title plus, only when useful, one much smaller uppercase supporting line in regular or light weight.
- Keep the supporting line more openly tracked than the title.
- Align type to the subject's dominant edge, direction, or negative-space field. Let it participate in the eye path rather than sit inside a label box.
- Use the current source-derived poster palette for type. Prefer one solid high-contrast color selected from that image's functional colors; never carry over a type color from a previous poster by habit. Avoid outlines, bevels, glows, shadows, gradients, chrome, extrusion, and distressed display effects.
- Do not enclose typography in a frame, panel, banner, capsule, grid box, or arbitrary color block.

## Copy rules

- Derive copy from the visible subject and poster proposition, never from invented facts.
- Default to one evocative English title of 1–3 words, ideally 4–16 letters total.
- Add at most one supporting line of 1–4 words when it improves hierarchy or meaning.
- Prefer concrete editorial language connected to motion, light, material, place, scale, atmosphere, or transformation.
- Never invent a brand, model name, location, event, date, specification, credit, slogan, or logo.
- Avoid generic filler such as `DESIGN POSTER`, `CREATIVE ART`, `FUTURE VISION`, and random serial numbers.
- Preserve user-supplied wording and capitalization exactly.

## Placement and breathing room

- Place the title in an intentional type zone with generous surrounding substrate or negative space.
- Allow asymmetric placement, vertical stacking, line breaks, or partial alignment with the focal form when it strengthens the composition.
- Keep the title fully readable; do not hide essential letters behind the subject or crop them accidentally.
- Maintain enough empty space that the type feels integrated and calm, not squeezed into a leftover corner.
- Do not repeat the title decoratively or scatter microtype across the canvas.

## Prompt vocabulary

Describe the treatment as: `bold condensed uppercase neo-grotesk sans serif, tall narrow proportions, clean even strokes, tight readable tracking, editorial scale contrast, integrated into active negative space, no text box, no outline, no shadow, no decorative effects`.

State the exact permitted words in the prompt and forbid all other lettering.

## Acceptance checks

- The primary title reads accurately at normal viewing size.
- The letterforms are condensed, uppercase, clean, and modern.
- The title and optional supporting line have a decisive size/weight contrast.
- Typography reinforces the source geometry and poster eye path.
- The type zone retains breathing room and is not boxed.
- No additional or corrupted lettering appears.
